# Renter Confidence Test: Session Script

**Draft: needs Timothy's OK before any session.**

## What we want to learn

- How quickly do renters understand whether the app is sure or unsure about each issue?
- Do they read a word ("Likely", "Possible", "Unsure") faster than looking at a visual bar?
- Which one would they trust more when deciding what to ask the agent about?

## Before you start

### Consent script (read this aloud)

"Thanks for helping us test the app. We're trying to understand how renters like you read information about things the app finds during an inspection. This will take about 10 minutes.

We'll show you two different ways to display the same information and ask you some questions about them. There's no right answer – we're just trying to learn what works best.

Your name won't be kept. We'll take notes about what you do and say so we can remember the session later, but only if you're happy with that. We won't record you or take any photos or video unless you say yes – and you can change your mind any time.

If anything doesn't make sense or you want to stop, just let me know. OK?"

### Setup checklist

- [ ] Phone is on the switcher or close-up stand so you can see the screen together
- [ ] Phone brightness is comfortable for the space you're in
- [ ] You've decided which design to show first (alternate between participants: P1 and P3 and P5 start with Words; P2 and P4 start with Scale)
- [ ] Notes table is ready for this participant
- [ ] You're ready to time each task roughly (a note like "30 seconds" is fine)

## Warm-up (1–2 minutes)

Ask these two questions to get them talking before you show anything:

1. "When was the last time you rented a place? How did the inspection go – was there anything the landlord or agent told you that surprised you?"
2. "Have you ever used an app to help you check a rental? What was that like?"

Listen but don't explain the app yet. You're just making them comfortable.

## Tasks (5–7 minutes per design)

For each design (Words first or Scale first, depending on the order you chose), ask the same four tasks in this order. Read the task line out loud. Let them do it without any help, but watch and take notes on what you see and hear.

Don't explain what the design means before they try it. Don't point at anything on the screen.

### Task 1: "Look at this screen. Tell me what you see."

**What to watch for:**
- Do they look at the whole screen or jump to one part?
- Do they mention the confidence words or bar at all?
- Do they read the issue description, the photo region, the confidence word or bar, or something else?
- Any hesitation or confusion about what they're looking at?

### Task 2: "Which of these things would you ask the agent about first? Why?"

Point at the screen if they're stuck, but don't point at the confidence word or bar yet.

**What to watch for:**
- Do they pick the "Likely" one first, or something else?
- Did they use the confidence word or bar to help them decide?
- Do they explain why in terms of risk, importance, or something else?
- How long does it take them to answer?

### Task 3: "How sure is the app about this one?" (Point to a "Possible" issue.)

**What to watch for:**
- Do they read the word or look at the bar?
- Do they understand what "Possible" or "2 steps" means? Any confusion?
- Can they say it back to you in their own words?

### Task 4: "Is there anything on this screen that the app isn't sure about?"

**What to watch for:**
- Can they spot the "Unsure" or low-filled bar without you pointing?
- Do they mention the confidence word or bar, or do they read something else?
- Any surprise that the app admits uncertainty?

## After both designs (2–3 minutes)

Ask these comparison questions:

1. "Which one was quicker to understand – the words or the bar?"
2. "Which one would you trust more if you were asking the agent questions?"
3. "Was anything confusing in either of them?"
4. "Is there anything else you'd want to see in a screen like this?"

Don't push them to choose; if they say "they're the same" or "I'm not sure", that's a real answer. Take their exact words if they said yes to notes at the start.

## Wrap-up and thanks (30 seconds)

"That's really helpful. We'll use what you've told us to make the app better. Thanks so much for your time."

## Rules for notes

- **Never put words in their mouth.** Write what they actually did and said, not what you think they meant.
- **Never invent a quote.** Only write exact words if they said yes to notes at the start. Otherwise, summarise what they did (e.g. "nodded", "pointed at the word").
- **Don't explain the designs before the tasks.** Let them see them cold so you can learn how they read them.
- **Phrase issues as questions, not claims.** If they get confused, write "Possible issue: they didn't notice the bar" rather than "They couldn't read it."
